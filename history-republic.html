People's Movement II & the Republic

2006 – 2008 CE (2062–2065 BS) · Jana Andolan II · The end of the monarchy

At a glance
  • The movement: 19 days of mass protest in April 2006, led by the Seven-Party Alliance with Maoist support.
  • Victory: on 24 April 2006 King Gyanendra reinstates parliament.
  • Key step: the proclamation of 18 May 2006 strips the king of his powers.
  • Peace and a new framework: the Comprehensive Peace Accord (Nov 2006) and the Interim Constitution (15 January 2007).
  • The republic: on 28 May 2008 the first Constituent Assembly abolishes the monarchy and declares Nepal a federal democratic republic.

1. How the movement began

After the king's direct rule of 2005, the Seven-Party Alliance and the Maoists agreed in Delhi (November 2005) to work together for democracy. In early April 2006 the alliance called for a nationwide general strike. Despite curfews and a shoot-on-sight order, hundreds of thousands of people poured into the streets of Kathmandu and other cities. It is called Jana Andolan II to separate it from the 1990 movement.

2. The 19 days of April
  • Protests grew daily, led by party workers but joined by lawyers, doctors, teachers, civil servants and ordinary families.
  • The security forces used force, and at least 18 people were killed and thousands injured by one count.
  • On 21 April the king offered to return power to the parties, but they rejected a deal that left him in charge.
  • On 24 April, with crowds nearing the palace, he reinstated the House of Representatives that had been dissolved in 2002.
3. The new government (April–May 2006)

Girija Prasad Koirala became prime minister. The restored parliament acted swiftly, with the Maoists declaring a three-month ceasefire on 26 April and the government reciprocating on 3 May. On 18 May 2006 it unanimously passed a proclamation that removed the king's authority, placed the army under parliament (the Royal Nepal Army became the Nepal Army), made Nepal a secular state, and promised a Constituent Assembly election.

4. Peace and the interim constitution
  • 21 November 2006: the Comprehensive Peace Accord ended the war.
  • 15 January 2007: the Interim Constitution, drafted by a committee headed by Justice Laxman Prasad Aryal, replaced the 1990 constitution. A 330-member interim parliament included the Maoists.
  • The Interim Constitution set Nepal as a secular state and committed it to a Constituent Assembly. The monarchy's fate was left to be decided by the first Assembly meeting.
  • The UN mission (UNMIN) monitored the arms of both sides.
5. The Madhesh Movement (2007)

In January–February 2007, protests broke out in the southern plains over a feeling that the Interim Constitution ignored the Madhesi people. They demanded federalism, a fair electoral system and better representation. The government agreed to amend the Interim Constitution and add federalism, and changed the electoral design to give more weight to proportional representation. The movement put identity-based federalism firmly on the national agenda.

6. Delays and the republic agreement

The Constituent Assembly election, first set for June 2007, was postponed twice. In September 2007 the Maoist ministers resigned over the demand to declare a republic in advance. After a compromise in December 2007, parliament agreed to abolish the monarchy, to be formalised by the first Assembly meeting, and the Maoists returned to the cabinet.

7. The 2008 Constituent Assembly election
  • Held on 10 April 2008 for a 601-member Assembly, with a mix of directly elected, proportional and nominated seats.
  • The CPN (Maoist) emerged as the largest party, though short of a majority, followed by the Nepali Congress and the CPN (UML).
  • The Assembly was more diverse than any earlier legislature, with many more women, Dalits, Janajati and Madhesi members.
  • It was given two years to write a constitution.
8. 28 May 2008: the republic

At its first meeting on 28 May 2008 the Assembly voted overwhelmingly to abolish the 240-year-old monarchy and declared Nepal a Federal Democratic Republic. King Gyanendra left the Narayanhiti Palace in June. Ram Baran Yadav (Nepali Congress) was elected the first president on 21 July 2008, and on 18 August Prachanda of the CPN (Maoist) became prime minister. 28 May is celebrated as Republic Day.

9. What came next

The first Assembly could not agree on how to organise federalism and extended its term several times before dissolving in May 2012. A second Assembly was elected in November 2013 and promulgated the new constitution on 20 September 2015, the subject of the final era.

Terms worth a closer look

Tap a dotted term anywhere above to see its meaning right there. This list is the same set, for reference.

Seven-Party Alliance (SPA)

The coalition of seven major parliamentary parties, including the Nepali Congress and CPN (UML), that led the 2006 movement and then formed the government after the king stepped back.

Proclamation of 18 May 2006

The unanimous declaration by the reinstated House of Representatives that ended the king's powers, brought the army under parliament, made Nepal secular, and began the road to a Constituent Assembly. It is often called Nepal's "Magna Carta" and marked as Loktantra Diwas.

Interim Constitution, 2007

A temporary constitution promulgated on 15 January 2007 to run the country until the Constituent Assembly wrote a permanent one. It was amended several times, including to add federalism and to declare the republic.

Madhesh Movement

The 2007 protests in Nepal's southern plains for federalism, fair representation and recognition of Madhesi identity. A second wave in 2015 followed the promulgation of the new constitution.

Constituent Assembly (CA)

An elected body whose main job is to write a new constitution. Nepal's first CA (2008) did not finish; the second (2013) promulgated the 2015 constitution.

Republic Day (Ganatantra Diwas)

Observed every 15 Jestha (28 May) in memory of the first Constituent Assembly meeting in 2008, when the monarchy was abolished.
