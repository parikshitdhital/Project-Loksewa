Ancient Nepal

c. 800 BCE – 879 CE · Kirat tradition · Lumbini & Ashoka · The Licchavi age

At a glance
  • Core region: the Kathmandu Valley, known as Nepal Mandala.
  • Three layers of evidence: chronicles (Vamsavalis), stone inscriptions, and foreign travellers' records.
  • Earliest dated inscription: King Manadeva, Changu Narayan, 464 CE.
  • Golden age: the Licchavi period, with Sanskrit records, fine stone sculpture and busy trade routes.
  • Buddha's birthplace: Lumbini, marked by Ashoka's pillar (c. 249 BCE).

1. How do we know? (Sources)

Chronicles. The Vamsavalis list kings from legendary times. They are valuable but written much later, so scholars treat their early dates cautiously.

Inscriptions. Stone and metal records, mostly Sanskrit deeds of gifts to temples, give dated, reliable facts. The Licchavi records run from the 5th to the 8th century.

Travellers. Chinese pilgrims and envoys (Faxian, Xuanzang, Wang Xuance) described Nepal's cities, kings, religion and trade.

Archaeology. Excavations at Lumbini and in the valley help test the written accounts.

2. The Kirat period (traditional)

Before the Licchavis, the chronicles name earlier dynasties, the Gopala and Mahispala, followed by the Kirat. The Kirat are linked with the eastern hills, and today's Rai, Limbu, Yakkha and Sunuwar communities trace their heritage to them.

  • The first Kirat king is named Yalambar; the chronicles list 29 kings in all.
  • The 7th king, Jitedasti, is said to have ruled in the Buddha's time, and Sthunko is said to have received Emperor Ashoka.
  • The last king, Gasti, is said to have fallen to the Licchavi leader Nimisha.

Caution for students: this account comes from late chronicles, and physical evidence for individual Kirat kings is limited. Exams usually ask the names above, not the dates.

3. Lumbini and the Shakya land

The Buddha was born into the Shakya clan, whose small republic centred on Kapilvastu, in the southern plains. Lumbini, in today's Rupandehi district, is his birthplace.

  • Ashoka's pillar (c. 249 BCE): a Brahmi inscription says Ashoka came here, that the Buddha was born here, and that the village was relieved of one tax and pays only an eighth share of the produce.
  • Rediscovery (1896): the pillar was identified by the German archaeologist Alois Führer with local governor Khadga Shumsher Rana.
  • 2013 excavation: archaeologists reported an early timber structure beneath the Mayadevi Temple, possibly as old as the 6th century BCE.
  • World Heritage: Lumbini was listed by UNESCO in 1997.
4. The Licchavi dynasty (c. 400–879)

The Licchavis ruled the Kathmandu Valley and nearby valleys. They are called Nepal's golden age for their records, art and trade. The name matches the famous Licchavis of Vaishali in India, though the link between the two is debated (see term).

  • Manadeva I (464–505): put down revolts in the east and west, issued Mananka coins, and built the Managriha palace.
  • Amshuverma (605–621): began as a Mahasamanta (great feudatory) and rose to the throne. He is linked with the Kailashkut Bhawan palace, his own coins, and, in Tibetan tradition, with the marriage of his daughter Bhrikuti to the Tibetan king Songtsen Gampo.
  • Narendradeva (643–679): fled to Tibet when his father Udayadeva was overthrown, then regained the throne with Tibetan help. He sent and received envoys with Tang China.
  • Jayadeva II: a later king; his 733 CE inscription at Pashupati preserves the royal line.
5. Government and society

The system resembled that of northern India. The king held grand titles and ruled with high officials, while powerful local lords, the Mahasamantas, controlled districts and sometimes rivalled the crown.

  • Villages were run by local assemblies; the term often given is Panchali.
  • Land was granted to temples and monasteries through endowments, the roots of the guthi system.
  • Records were written in Sanskrit, mostly using the Licchavi-era script.
6. Economy and trade

Farming fed the valley, but trade made it rich. Nepal lay on routes between India and Tibet, so caravans paid customs duties and carried goods, ideas and artists across the Himalaya.

  • Coins such as Mananka show a monetary economy.
  • Nepali craft and cloth were known abroad.
  • Trade also carried Buddhism north to Tibet.
7. Religion, art and architecture

Shaivism, Vaishnavism and Buddhism existed side by side; kings supported several, and inscriptions record gifts to all.

  • Pashupatinath: Shiva worship at Deopatan already appears in Licchavi records.
  • Changu Narayan: the valley's oldest surviving temple complex, with the finest early stone carvings and Manadeva's pillar.
  • Budhanilkantha: the large reclining Vishnu figure, usually attributed to the 7th–8th century.
  • Licchavi palaces and public buildings have not survived; the stone sculptures and inscriptions have.
8. Foreign relations: Gupta, Tibet, China
  • Guptas (India): Samudragupta's inscription lists Nepala as a border kingdom paying tribute and obeying orders; Nepal was a smaller neighbour in India's orbit at this time.
  • Tibet: as Tibet grew strong in the 7th century, the two states exchanged marriages, troops and religion.
  • China: Tang envoys travelled via Tibet; relations soured briefly after a mission was mistreated, and Narendradeva and Songtsen Gampo sent troops to respond.
9. Decline and the road to 879

After Narendradeva, strong feudatories and outside pressure weakened the Licchavi crown. Records become fewer after the 8th century. A new calendar, Nepal Sambat, began in 879 CE; it is linked in tradition with the merchant Sankhadhar Sakhwa, and marks the move into the Medieval period covered in the next era.

Exam focus

  • Earliest dated inscription in Nepal: Manadeva, Changu Narayan, 464 CE.
  • First named coin: Mananka (Manadeva I).
  • First Kirat king: Yalambar. Number of Kirat kings: 29. Last: Gasti.
  • Licchavi king who defeated the last Kirat king (in the chronicles): Nimisha.
  • Ashoka's Lumbini pillar: about 249 BCE; UNESCO listing: 1997.
  • Amshuverma: rose from Mahasamanta; linked with Kailashkut Bhawan; Bhrikuti tradition.
  • Narendradeva: regained throne with Tibetan help; reigned 643–679.
  • Nepal Sambat begins: 879 CE.
  • Nepali name of the valley in this age: Nepal Mandala.

Terms worth a closer look

Tap a dotted term anywhere above to see its meaning right there. This list is the same set, for reference.

Vamsavali (chronicle)

A genealogical chronicle of kings, copied and extended over centuries. The Gopalarajavamsavali is the best known. Useful for the order of dynasties, but it mixes legend with history.

Nepal Mandala

The old name for the Kathmandu Valley and its surrounding region, meaning the cultural and political "circle" of Nepal. "Nepal" meant this valley long before it meant the whole country.

Mananka

Silver coins issued under Manadeva I, usually described as the first named coinage of Nepal. They show the valley had a monetary economy by the 5th century.

Mahasamanta

A "great feudatory": a lord who governed a region and owed loyalty to the king but held real military and local power. Amshuverma used this position to rise to the throne.

Guthi / Gosthi

A trust or association that manages land and income for a temple, festival or community. Its earliest forms appear in Licchavi inscriptions, and the Newar guthi system developed from them.

Kailashkut Bhawan

Amshuverma's famous palace, described in Chinese accounts as a splendid building. It has not survived, and its exact site is debated.

Changu Narayan inscription

A stone pillar near the Changu Narayan temple, east of Bhaktapur, dated 464 CE. It records the victories of Manadeva I and is the oldest dated inscription found in Nepal. Its dating system (Saka 386) is the same era used for many Licchavi records.

Licchavi: one name, two places?

The Licchavis of Vaishali (India) are known from early Buddhist texts, and the Gupta dynasty claimed marriage ties to them. The Licchavis of Nepal ruled a local Kathmandu kingdom. Historians discuss whether the Nepal line descended from, or simply took the name of, the Indian clan.
